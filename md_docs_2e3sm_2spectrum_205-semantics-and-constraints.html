E3SM-SPEC: semantics and constraints

Units, clocks, the meaning of absent and zero, the limits of a PRB block, the checks that a receiver makes, and what happens when a message is wrong.

This is clause 9 of the specification. The fields are in clause 6. The procedures are in clause 7.

Clocks

Two clocks exist in the service model. They shall not be mixed.

  • The sensing indication and the cell configuration indication use the monotonic clock. It has a common origin for all processes on one host and no relation to the calendar. The sensing indication only has a meaning on the host of the RAN, because it holds a reference to local shared memory.
  • The dApp report, the xApp control and the apply outcome use the realtime clock. Each of them crosses from one host to another. The RAN relays the first two without a change.

A receiver shall not subtract a monotonic value from a realtime value, or the reverse. The two share the field name timestamp in the grammar, but they are not on the same clock.

An xApp can subtract the timestamp of a dApp report from installedTimestamp and from onAirTimestamp, because they are on the realtime clock. The accuracy of the result is the accuracy of the time synchronization between the hosts.

The latency recorder uses the monotonic clock. See latrec. A payload timestamp and a recorder timestamp shall not be subtracted.

A receiver shall not reject a report or an xApp control only because it has no timestamp.

A timestamp of "0" in an apply outcome is never sent. The RAN omits the field instead. A sender that has no value shall omit the field.

PRB block semantics

  • The PRB list is complete and not incremental. The RAN replaces its block with the new list.
  • An empty list clears the block.
  • The RAN applies the list to uplink and downlink. The message has no direction field.
  • The RAN blocks all 14 symbols of every listed PRB.
  • A PRB that appears twice in the list is blocked once.
  • The scheduler applies the block from the start of a following slot. It marks the PRBs as occupied in its resource map, so that every allocation step schedules around them.
  • The validityPeriod of the control is accepted. The RAN does not use it. A dApp that needs a block to expire shall send a clearing control itself.

Limits of a PRB block

A PRB block controls the allocation of the scheduler. It does not control the radio. The following limits apply. A dApp and an operator shall know them.

  1. The block is uniform. The RAN blocks every listed PRB for every channel. It does not protect or steer any channel. When a block hits a fixed PUCCH or SRS resource of a UE, the RAN logs the collision and does not move the resource.
  2. Cell common channels are not protected. The block takes effect on SSB, CORESET 0, PRACH and the cell common PUCCH. A connected UE tolerates a short block. A block over the downlink SSB or CORESET 0 region stops a new UE from synchronizing. A dApp should keep its list off these PRBs.
  3. A dedicated PUCCH or SRS under a block is dropped. The RAN skips the reception without an error. The UE loses scheduling requests, periodic CSI or SRS while the block overlaps, and recovers after it lifts.
  4. CSI-RS still transmits on blocked downlink PRBs. The resource map is an allocation tracker. It does not gate the transmission.
  5. A long block can stall a retransmission. A retransmission keeps its transport block size. If a block covers the only PRBs that it can use, the scheduler retries until the inactivity timeout removes the UE. The RAN has no guard for this case.
  6. Reception is not gated. A UE that transmits on a blocked uplink PRB is still received. The silence of a blocked PRB is best effort.
  7. Configured grants are not covered. The RAN does not implement configured grant scheduling. If it does, a UE with a configured grant transmits on its own PRBs, regardless of the block.
  8. A very high subcarrier spacing needs care. The cost of a block change grows with the size of the resource ring. It is small at 30 kHz. At millimeter wave spacing it can approach the slot deadline.
  9. Released PRBs return late. After a block shrinks, freed uplink PRBs can stay marked for about one ring cycle, which is 10 to 20 ms at 30 kHz.

Sensing policy semantics

  • A policy has one 14 bit mask for each slot of a frame. Bit s set means: the uplink scheduler prefers allocations that do not use symbol s of that slot for the UE.
  • The policy only states a preference. The scheduler can still use a masked symbol when it has no other allocation.
  • deactivate "TRUE" clears the policy and the masks are ignored.
  • The number of masks shall equal the number of slots in a frame of the RAN.
  • The validityPeriod of the policy is accepted. The RAN does not use it. A policy does not expire.

Validation

A receiver validates a message before it acts. The RAN applies these checks.

Table: Checks of the RAN on a control

Check Result when it fails
the service model runs negative acknowledge
the control has at least one octet negative acknowledge
the control identifier is "1" or "2" negative acknowledge
the MAC scheduler exists negative acknowledge
the payload decodes in the encoding of the run negative acknowledge
the payload holds the alternative that matches the control identifier negative acknowledge
the PRB list has at most 273 entries and every index is at most 272 negative acknowledge
maskPerSlot has 1 to 160 entries, and every value is at most 16383 negative acknowledge
the number of masks equals the slots in a frame of the RAN negative acknowledge
the scheduler accepts the new block or policy negative acknowledge

The RAN answers every control with an E3 Message Ack. The response code is "positive" or "negative". The acknowledge says only whether the RAN applied the control to the scheduler. It does not say that the block is in the air. The apply outcome says that.

A negative acknowledge does not change the state that was in force before the control.

Unknown identifiers and alternatives

  • A control identifier other than "1" or "2" is rejected with a negative acknowledge.
  • A choice alternative that the receiver does not know is rejected. The receiver shall not guess what it means. In APER, a receiver that does not know an extension alternative cannot read its content.
  • A receiver ignores an extension field of a structure that it does not know, where the encoding allows it. This holds for the apply outcome and for the structures with an extension marker.
  • A RAN function ID other than "1" in a control that reaches this service model is rejected.

Concurrency and freshness of the ring

  • The RAN has one writer for the sensing ring. No lock exists. The RAN writes a ring slot completely before it sends the indication that points to it.
  • The RAN writes sfn and slot of the ring slot last. A dApp compares them with the indication. A mismatch means that the slot was overwritten, and the dApp shall drop it.
  • A dApp reads the ring read only. It shall not write to it.

What is mandatory
